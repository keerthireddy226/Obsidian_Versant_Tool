## Combined takeaways, overall

**Copy these ideas:**
- Show a short instructions-plus-example screen before every new question type, every time (VersantPrep)
- Control timers from your own server, not the browser, and lock out tab-switching during a timed question (Mocko)
- Process audio/text right in the browser instead of uploading it, wherever realistic (MyEnglishPractice.jp)
- Never use multiple-choice for a serious test — always require a real typed or spoken answer (Mocko)
- Compare scores to real hiring numbers, not just an abstract score (VersantPrep)
- Meet your audience where they already are — job boards, WhatsApp groups, recruiter channels — not just search engines (VersantPro)
- Frame everything around the real job/test, not generic language learning (SpeakShark, Elispeak, Practice Me)
- Make it possible to try at least one real question with zero signup friction (MockVersant)
- Track small hesitation signals, like backspace counts while typing, as an extra feedback data point (MockVersant)
- Offer a path toward the real test booking inside your own product, and consider a certificate option for employer customers (VERSANT Bridge)
- A clear pre-test checklist — mic check, don't refresh, submit only when ready — builds trust before the test even starts (CA Monk)
- Be openly honest about what your scoring can't yet do well, rather than quietly overselling (Mocko)

**Avoid these mistakes:**
- Feedback text that doesn't check the real score before saying something positive (VersantPrep's bug)
- Required inputs with no working fallback, creating a permanent dead end (Skillioma)
- Free tiers that are blocked behind a paywall anyway (CA Monk's coins)
- Fake popularity counters or suspicious-looking reviews (VersantPro)
- Ever claiming or implying you're "official" or Pearson-approved (MockVersant)
- Letting users adjust how strict their own scoring is (Praktika)
- Generic, copy-paste-feeling feedback that ignores what the person actually answered (Skillioma)

**The gap that's still wide open:**
Every single platform found — all ~29 of them, general and Versant-specific — is built for **one person practicing alone**. Not one is built for a **company** to use when hiring many people at once: no batch dashboards for employers, no serious handling of many candidates' personal data, no B2B pricing model, no trustworthy certificate/report for hiring decisions. See [[Market Gap & Positioning]] for what this means for the product's actual strategy, and [[What To Take — Design Lessons]] for the fully consolidated do's/don'ts list this guide feeds into.
