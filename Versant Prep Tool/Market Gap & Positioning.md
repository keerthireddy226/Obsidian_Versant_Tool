# Market Gap & Positioning

> [[00 - Index|← Back to Index]]

Why the product is positioned around **employers**, not individual learners, and what the competitive research actually proved versus what it left open.

---

## The claim that got tested and killed

[[BRD — Versant Prep Tool]] originally carried **Assumption A3**: "no incumbent AI-based Versant preparation product holds the market." Direct search and hands-on evaluation found **at least 14 Versant-specific practice sites/apps** — [[VERSANT Bridge — Competitor Deep-Dive|VERSANT Bridge]], [[Versant Prep Sites — Walkthrough|VersantPrep, CA Monk, Skillioma, TDS Global Solutions]], plus Mocko, MockVersant, VersantPro, MyEnglishPractice.jp (see [[Versant Sites — Quick Reference Directory]]) — plus Pearson's own official channel.

**A3 is marked resolved false as of 2026-08-25.** The paired **Risk R6** ("an incumbent product already owns the niche") is closed the same day, with an explicit resolution: **reposition, don't stop.**

## What "reposition" means concretely

Every one of those ~14+ sites (and every general AI speaking app reviewed — see [[AI Speaking Apps — Landscape]]) shares the same shape: **built for one individual, practicing alone, paying for themselves (or not paying at all).** None of them have, or show any sign of building:

- An employer-facing dashboard showing how a whole batch of candidates scored
- Serious handling of many candidates' personal data and recordings at scale, under a compliance regime
- A B2B pricing/licensing model (per-seat, per-cohort) instead of individual subscriptions
- A certificate or report an employer can actually trust for a hiring decision

**The gap isn't the product category — it's the geography and the buyer.** VERSANT Bridge is the clearest proof: a real, multi-test, subscription business — but built for individual working adults in Japan paying in yen. TDS Global Solutions proves the B2B buyer already exists and already spends money in this exact space — it just isn't a practice tool at all, purely a Pearson-partnered sales funnel straight to enterprise deals.

## Who the buyer actually is — the unresolved question underneath the resolved one

Closing A3/R6 didn't resolve the harder question the BRD keeps as its own **Assumption A1 ("highest risk in this document")**: even granting the B2B angle is the right positioning, *who* is the reachable buyer?

Versant is employer-administered — a candidate cannot self-book a real Versant test, a Test Identification Number is issued by a test administrator, and results go to the purchasing organisation, not the candidate. That structurally rules out "sell directly to individual candidates" as the primary channel the way VersantPrep, VERSANT Bridge, and the rest all do. The BRD identifies four possible buyers with very different willingness and ability to pay:

| Possible buyer | What they'd want |
|---|---|
| The employer directly | Reduced hiring cost per qualified candidate — batch readiness visibility before spending on the real test |
| A staffing agency | Screen candidates before submitting them to a client |
| A training academy (e.g. Edstellar, the company behind this BRD) | Integration with existing L&D offerings, a sellable proposition into existing client relationships |
| The candidate | Individual improvement — the same buyer every existing competitor already serves |

This is why BRD Assumption A1 requires CRM queries and 8-10 structured client conversations before Phase 1 build spend, not just external market signal. A validated external signal was found — TDS Global Solutions confirms real companies pay for Versant-adjacent services today — but that is explicitly *not* a substitute for internal evidence that Edstellar's own existing relationships include a reachable buyer.

## Certification and trust — the pattern to lean into

Two competitors independently point at the same lever: **VERSANT Bridge** offers corporate completion certificates on request; **VersantPro**'s WhatsApp job-board channel proves employers/recruiters are already a live audience for Versant-adjacent content. Neither treats the employer as the *primary* customer the way this product's B2B framing does — but both prove that employer-facing trust artifacts (certificates, batch visibility) are a real, wanted thing once you look past the individual-learner default every competitor ships.

## What still can't be claimed

- Scale of demand is unverified — BRD Appendix B logs "Pearson delivers ~3 million Versant tests annually" and "Philippine 4-Skills mean score ≈63" as **unverified** secondary claims, not to be relied on for planning.
- VERSANT Bridge's "2,000 learners, real score gains" is a disclosed, hedged claim from *their* pre-release testing — not independent evidence transferable to this product.
- Whether a training-academy buyer (Edstellar's own existing model) or a direct-employer buyer is the right one remains genuinely open — this note describes the shape of the gap, not which specific buyer fills it.
