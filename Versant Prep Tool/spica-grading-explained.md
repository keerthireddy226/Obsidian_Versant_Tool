# What happens to your answer

A simple explanation of what happens when you skip, answer wrong, or answer right — and how the whole test works.

### SKIP You skip a question

**Next question:** a bit easier than the one you skipped.

**Does it count against you?** Yes — skipping is treated the same as getting it wrong, since you didn't attempt it.

**Does it affect your final level?** A skipped question is never counted as "correct," so it can only pull your result down, never help it. If you skip too much of one skill, that skill may just show "not enough answered" instead of a level.

### WRONG You answer, but it's wrong

**Next question:** a bit easier than the one you got wrong.

**Does it count against you?** Yes, same as a skip — one level step down for that question type.

**Does it affect your final level?** Yes — this is a real, counted answer, so it's used when working out your level for that skill.

### RIGHT You answer, and it's correct

**Next question:** a bit harder than the one you just got right.

**Does it count for you?** Yes — it's real evidence you can handle that difficulty.

**Does it affect your final level?** Yes — enough correct answers at a level is what actually earns you that level in your result.

**One thing right vs wrong doesn't depend on: how you sound.** If you're speaking your answer, how clearly or fluently you say it is checked separately — but only *after* what you said is already correct. If what you said is wrong, it's just marked wrong, no matter how well you said it. Good delivery can never turn a wrong answer into a right one — it can only make an already-right answer count a little less if it was very hard to understand.

**What if our AI grading itself glitches (not your fault)?** Every so often, the system that checks spoken/written answers can have a technical hiccup (like a brief outage) — that's different from you being wrong. In that rare case, your next question now stays at the *same* difficulty, not a harder or easier one. It used to unfairly treat that glitch the same as a wrong answer — that's fixed now.

**Putting it together — a real-style sequence:**

1. You start a "Read Aloud" question at the middle level (B1).
2. You skip it → next Read Aloud question is one level easier (A2).
3. You answer that one, but it's wrong → next one is easier again (A1).
4. You answer that one correctly → next one goes back up a level (A2).
5. This keeps adjusting to find roughly where you're comfortable — that final settling point is what becomes your level for that skill.

## How questions are picked

The rules behind what question you get next.

- Every test starts in the **middle level** — not too easy, not too hard — so it's fair to everyone.
- Right answer → next question of that **same type** is a little harder.
- Wrong answer or skip → next question of that same type is a little easier.
- You can never go below the easiest level or above the hardest level.
- Each question type (like "Read Aloud" or "Answer a Question") moves up and down **on its own** — doing well in one type doesn't help or hurt another type.
- You only get a **small, fixed number** of questions per type — usually just 1 to 3. Once those are used up, the test automatically moves to the next type, no matter how you did.
- The exact question is chosen with a little randomness, but it always tries to give you one you haven't seen before.

## How answers are graded

What actually happens when you hit submit.

- Every answer is checked **immediately** when you submit it — never later.
- Simple answers (multiple choice, typing, fill-in-the-blank) are checked by a straightforward rule: does it match the correct answer or not.
- Open answers (writing or speaking) are checked by AI — it reads what you wrote or said and judges if it genuinely answers the question well, based on what's expected for that difficulty level.
- For totally open questions (like "tell me about yourself"), there's no single fixed answer — the AI just checks if you actually gave a real, relevant answer, not whether you said a specific phrase.
- If you're speaking, your pronunciation and fluency are checked **separately** from what you said:
    - If what you said is wrong, pronunciation and fluency don't matter at all — it's simply wrong.
    - If what you said is right, good pronunciation keeps it right — but very poor pronunciation or pace can still turn it into "wrong."
- Sometimes grading can't get a clean result:
    - **Skipped** → counted as wrong.
    - **Grading system glitch** (rare, not your fault) → doesn't count as right or wrong — you just get another question at the same level. *(Just fixed — it used to unfairly count as wrong.)*

**Still open / not fully solved:** the pronunciation check has no built-in rule to ignore accents — so a strong regional accent could theoretically get scored lower, even if every word is correct. This hasn't been tested yet with real varied-accent recordings.

## How your level is worked out

Turning all those answers into a level like A1, B2, or C1.

- Levels go from easiest to hardest: **A1 → A2 → B1 → B2 → C1 → C2**.
- Getting "credit" for a level needs a minimum accuracy, and that minimum gets stricter the higher the level:

| Level | Accuracy needed |
| --- | --- |
| A1 / A2 | 60% |
| B1 | 65% |
| B2 | 70% |
| C1 | 85% |
| C2 | 95% (almost perfect) |

- Each skill — Listening, Speaking, Reading, Writing — gets its **own** level, worked out separately.
- Only questions that were actually graded (not skipped, not glitched) count toward a skill's level.
