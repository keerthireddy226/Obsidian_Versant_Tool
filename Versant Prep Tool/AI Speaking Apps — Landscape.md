# AI Speaking Apps — Landscape & Versant Relevance

> [[00 - Index|← Back to Index]]

Quick-reference comparison of 15 general AI speaking-practice apps (not Versant-specific), covering how each works, pricing/free-access reality, whether it's actually good, and whether it helps with Versant prep. Companion to [[TalkPal — UX Engagement Analysis]] (the only app in this list tested hands-on by us; the rest are secondary-sourced).

---

## The core finding

**Almost every app runs the same model: free to sign up, not free to use.** What looks like a free tier is usually one narrow taste (one bot, a few minutes, a few sessions) — everything the app is actually good for sits behind Premium. Confirmed directly for TalkPal (free tier = one chatbot only, zero practice modes) and for Promova (a "free trial" that's actually a paid week that auto-bills).

**Free-access legend:** ✅ true free tier · 🔒 free tier exists but very limited · ⏳ no-card trial that expires · 💳 card-required trial (auto-bills) · 🧪 one-off free tool only · ❌ paid only · ❓ unverified marketing claim

|---|---|---|---|---|

**Bottom line:** every one is not helpful for actual Versant prep, but SpeakShark, Elispeak, and Practice Me are closest in *audience* — built for people prepping for jobs/interviews/exams, same population Versant tests. None replicate its timed, verbatim-scored task types.

### Why the free-access column got corrected twice

First pass called several apps "freemium" too loosely from marketing copy. Second, bigger correction: "no card required at signup" turned out to be a nearly meaningless signal on its own — a real user of the TalkPal account tested it directly and found the free tier only unlocks one chatbot, none of the modes/tests/scoring the whole comparison is based on. Gliglish, SpeakShark, and Elispeak's free-tier claims are still just marketing copy, unverified the way TalkPal's now is.

## What to take from this, for your own tool

**Worth copying:**
- Layered feedback (quick pass/fail, deeper "why" only on request) — TalkPal does this best. Use in Coach mode only, never Exam mode.
- Word-by-word pronunciation scoring, not one overall number — TalkPal's version is the best example found.
- Rotate which items show up each day, to fight repetition.
- No visible transcript during audio playback — Mintza's design. Versant's Repeats task is audio-only with nothing to read; Coach mode should train that way too.
- Frame around the real job/scenario, not generic language learning — SpeakShark/Elispeak's approach.

**Worth avoiding:**
- Never hide the correct answer after a wrong attempt (TalkPal's tile-builder flaw).
- Don't tie progress/levels to time spent instead of accuracy.
- Don't let users adjust scoring strictness (Praktika's soft/strict dial) — your scoring must match Versant's rubric exactly.
- Don't let failures happen silently (Univerbal's mic bugs, TalkPal's unclear wrong-answer states) — a silent failure during a real scored attempt is much worse than in a casual app.
- Budget extra for reliability — two crashes in one casual TalkPal session would ruin a real Exam-mode result.

**Nothing to copy — build from scratch:**
- Dictation (hear a sentence, type it in exactly 25s) — no app in this set does this.
- Passage Reconstruction (hear a passage, retell it, get scored) — nothing to reference.
- Sentence Builds done the real way (hear 3 spoken chunks, say them back in order, under time pressure) — TalkPal's tile version is tap-to-reorder, not spoken-under-time.
